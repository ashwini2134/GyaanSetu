Product Plan · No Code

The Fraylon Academy Landing Page, Rethought

A section-by-section audit of what's live today, followed by a complete narrative redesign — sitemap, visitor journey, copy, animation direction, and component recommendations. No implementation. Grounded in the actual current codebase, not a generic template.

Scope13 live sections + 10 unused/orphaned components read in full
Core problemReads like "another LMS," not an engineering academy

00 / Before the section audit

The page's real problem isn't any one section

It's the shape of the whole thing. These eight patterns showed up while reading through all thirteen live sections plus the ten that were built and abandoned — they explain why the page feels generic more than any single weak section does.

01

The same four ideas, six times

"Real projects, not videos," "AI reviews your code," "AI mentor," and "portfolio" each get restated in Hero, What Is Fraylon, Problem/Solution, Problem Cards, How It Works, Platform Spotlight, and Testimonials — with less novelty every time. By section seven a returning idea reads as filler, not reinforcement.

02

The flagship idea has no section

Portfolio is Fraylon's most ownable, most different claim — and today it's a bullet point in three other sections. A full LearnerPortfolioSection component already exists in the codebase, fully built, and is never imported anywhere.

03

Template fatigue

Nine of thirteen sections use the identical formula: small mono eyebrow → bold heading → one sentence → card grid with a hover shadow. That repetition of rhythm, not just message, is a bigger tell than any copy choice — it's why the page reads as a template even where the writing is good.

04

One stock photo, doing a lot of damage

The Courses section uses generic Unsplash photography — a laptop, a circuit board — while every other section is custom-coded. It's the single most "default LMS" moment on the page, sitting right next to the most product-specific one.

05

The methodology story exists but is buried

HowItWorksSection is already 80% of the "Learn → Practice → Build → Portfolio" story the brief asks for — but it's positioned as item five of thirteen in a scroll, not staged as its own moment.

06

Two taglines, no canonical line

Hero says "Don't Just Watch. Build. Ship. Get Hired." Footer says "The LMS that grades itself. Learn. Build. Grow." Neither is wrong, but a visitor who reads both isn't sure which one is the Fraylon line.

07

The stats undercut confidence

Leading the social-proof stat row with "2 Project-Based Courses" is honest, but it's the wrong honest number to lead with — it reads as small before a visitor has any other context to weigh it against.

08

Ten abandoned attempts, still in the repo

CommunitySection, FeaturesSection, LearningPathsSection, WhyFraylonSection, and six more exist, unused, each a past pass at this same pitch. None were deleted when superseded — evidence the page has never had one committed direction. This plan should end that pattern, not add an eleventh attempt.

03 / Told in order

Visitor Journey

What a first-time visitor thinks at each point in the scroll — the test for whether this is a story or a stack of sections.

0–3 seconds

"Oh — this is different."

Headline + a live-feeling build happening in front of them, not a stock photo or an abstract animation. This is the moment that decides whether they scroll or bounce.

~10%

"Okay, and it's legitimate."

Three quiet credibility marks land together, not one at a time. No claim feels oversold because nothing is a rotating banner.

~20–30%

"I see exactly what's different, once."

One scroll-driven transformation — video player literally becomes a live code + AI review panel — makes the old-way/new-way argument in a single motion instead of three separate sections repeating it.

~35–45%

"I understand how a lecture actually works."

The methodology loop — Learn → Interact → Practice → Solve → Build → Reflect → Certificate — plays out as a guided scroll, using real widgets from the actual product (the same Truth Table Explorer, Modus Ponens flashcards, and K-Means demo built into the live course).

~50%

"Wait, I can try this right now?"

A real, working practice exercise sits inline on the page. No login. This is the section most competitors cannot copy, because most don't have a real in-house code runner. Fraylon does.

~60–70%

"This is the part that actually matters to me."

The portfolio pipeline — the emotional peak of the page. Every exercise they'd just tried becomes a visible artifact flowing into a growing, real-looking portfolio. This is where "I should sign up" turns from interest into intent.

~75–85%

"Here's exactly what I'd study."

Roadmap and course catalog, now read as confirmation of a decision already being made, not persuasion.

~90%

"My last two questions, answered."

Capability grid resolves "is this a real platform" (certificates, tracking, mentor uptime); FAQ resolves anything specific still open, including a new portfolio-specific question.

100%

"Let's go."

Final CTA closes on the same visual idea the Hero opened with — a portfolio card settling into place — so the page ends where its argument was always heading.

Visual story · flagship 1 of 2

Teaching Methodology Loop

Every lecture in the product already follows this loop — it's not aspirational copy, it's what the converted lectures (1 through 9) actually do. The section's job is to make a visitor feel this loop, not read a list of it.

1
Learn
Short concept, plain text & diagrams
2
Interact
Animation or simulation — the "bulb," the quantifier scanner
3
Practice
Try It Yourself, in a real editor
4
Solve
A graded problem, not a quiz
5
Build
A real project or milestone
6
Reflect in Portfolio
The finished work is saved, not graded and discarded
7
Earn Certificate
Verifiable, tied to the work behind it

Animated as a pinned, horizontally-scrubbed timeline: the visitor scrolls vertically, the loop advances horizontally, and each stage swaps a small live preview panel behind it (real widget screenshots, not icons).

07 / Direction, not spectacle

Animation Plan

Grouped by trigger, so motion serves the story instead of decorating it.

Load-once

Hero sequence

Headline reveal → code-typing → portfolio-card resolve. Plays once on first paint, never repeats on scroll-back.

Scroll-triggered

Reveals & sticky moments

The Shift's morph, the Methodology horizontal scrub, the Portfolio pipeline's artifact-docking — all tied to scroll position, all reversible on scroll-up.

Ambient

Background glows & float

Kept from the current page — the blurred blob drift and card micro-float already work and shouldn't be touched, just used more sparingly so they read as atmosphere, not filler.

Interaction-driven

Hover & tilt

The mouse-tilt card effect (currently on Problem Cards, Stats, Testimonials, Courses) consolidates onto fewer sections — Courses and the Portfolio artifact cards — so it reads as a signature move, not wallpaper.

One-time-special

Confetti

Fires exactly once, on the Final CTA. Nowhere else. Using it more than once anywhere on the page cancels its effect.

08 / In one breath

Final UX Story

A visitor lands and watches something get built, not narrated. One clean scroll shows them what changed versus a normal course platform, told once instead of three times. Then they're shown — not told — how a lecture actually works, using the real interactive pieces already living inside the product. They try one, themselves, before they've made any commitment at all.

Then comes the idea the whole page has been building toward: everything they'd just done doesn't disappear into a gradebook. It becomes a portfolio, automatically, the whole way from a first practice exercise to a capstone project to an actual career outcome. That's the moment intent forms. Everything after — roadmap, courses, capabilities, FAQ — is confirmation, not persuasion. The page closes exactly where it opened: a portfolio card settling into place, proof that the promise from the first three seconds was kept.
